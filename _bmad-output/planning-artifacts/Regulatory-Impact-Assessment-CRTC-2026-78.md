# Regulatory Impact Assessment

## Telecom Regulatory Policy CRTC 2026-78 — “Enhancing self-service mechanisms”

**Assessment date:** 30 October 2026  
**Source:** [Telecom Regulatory Policy CRTC 2026-78](../../source/telecom%20regulatory%20policy%20CRTC%202026-78.pdf), issued 24 April 2026  
**Scope:** Business and customer impacts of the policy’s self-service requirements for telecommunications service providers.

> This assessment summarizes the policy and identifies likely implementation implications. It is not legal advice. The policy text and applicable legislation take precedence.

## Executive Summary

CRTC 2026-78 requires telecommunications service providers to offer customers an easy-to-use self-service mechanism to modify or cancel service plans without interacting with a live customer service representative. The mechanism must support informed, express consent; actions should take effect immediately to the greatest extent possible; and the provider must send written confirmation of every self-service action and state the expected confirmation timeframe in the mechanism.

The requirements apply to all service providers and are to be enforced starting **26 April 2027**. The CRTC will administer them directly rather than incorporating them into the Wireless Code or Internet Code or assigning them to the Commission for Complaints for Telecom-television Services (CCTS). The policy also prohibits fees for accessing any type of customer service. It does not prohibit all charges associated with a transaction: it notes that fees for actions may still be charged, subject to other applicable determinations.

For customers, the intended impact is simpler control over service plans, improved transparency, and documented proof of requested changes. For providers, compliance likely requires coordinated changes to digital customer journeys, plan-management systems, confirmation and recordkeeping processes, customer support, and compliance oversight. The policy allows flexibility in the mechanism and does not specify a single channel, a fixed confirmation deadline, or detailed technical standards.

## Regulatory Objectives

The policy aims to:

- Implement the Telecommunications Act’s requirement for self-service mechanisms that let subscribers modify or cancel telecommunications service plans (paras. 3–4; pp. 2–3).
- Make it easier for customers to change or cancel service, reducing barriers to switching and supporting competition (para. 11; p. 4).
- Advance consumer protection, affordability, and customer control while leaving providers flexibility to choose mechanisms suited to their circumstances (paras. 12–16; pp. 4–5).
- Promote transparent, informed, and express consent for plan changes and cancellations (paras. 14–15; p. 4).
- Improve accountability by requiring written confirmation of self-service actions (paras. 28–29; pp. 6–7).
- Give providers implementation time before enforcement begins on 26 April 2027 (para. 39; p. 8).

## Compliance Obligations

| Obligation | Policy requirement | Scope / timing |
|---|---|---|
| Provide self-service | Make an easy-to-use mechanism available that enables customers to modify or cancel their telecommunications service plan without interacting with a live customer service representative. Examples include an app, website, or email. | All service providers; enforcement starts 26 April 2027 (paras. 16, 38–39, 43; pp. 5, 8–9). The definition applies to individual and small-business customers (para. 17; p. 5). |
| Support plan changes | Enable plan modifications, including upgrades, downgrades, and adding or removing services, as well as cancellation. | Mechanism must allow customers to act on their own (paras. 13, 16; pp. 4–5). |
| Avoid unnecessary representative interaction | Do not require a live representative to complete the action, unless additional information is reasonably required to clarify the customer’s intent. | The policy’s definition and analysis set this expectation (para. 13; p. 4). |
| Make actions timely | To the greatest extent possible, actions taken through self-service must take effect immediately. | No specific processing-time metric is set in the policy (para. 13; p. 4). |
| Obtain informed, express consent | Make the process transparent and allow customers to give informed and express consent. For a plan change, make the customer aware of resulting contract changes and whether they can return to their existing terms if dissatisfied. | Paras. 14–15; p. 4. |
| Confirm each action in writing | Deliver written confirmation to the customer for any action taken through self-service. State within the mechanism how long the confirmation is expected to take. | Applies to any self-service action; no universal confirmation deadline is specified (paras. 28–29; pp. 6–7; para. 44; p. 9). |
| Do not charge for access to customer service | Do not charge customers a fee to access any type of customer service. | The policy distinguishes access fees from fees for actions taken; it notes action-related fees may still be charged, subject to other CRTC determinations (paras. 26–27; p. 6). |
| Apply existing accessibility and privacy protections | Existing accessibility and privacy protections continue to apply to self-service mechanisms. | The CRTC did not impose additional requirements in this proceeding; it encouraged providers to make mechanisms accessible and not rely exclusively on live support for customers requiring accommodations (paras. 30–31, 45; pp. 7, 9). |
| Prepare for direct CRTC administration | Self-service requirements will not be integrated into the Codes or administered by the CCTS; the Commission will administer the requirement, including receiving customer complaints and ensuring provider availability of the mechanism. | Paras. 35–38; p. 8. |

## Customer Experience Impacts

**Expected benefits**

- Customers should be able to upgrade, downgrade, add or remove services, and cancel without navigating a live-representative interaction.
- More timely plan changes are expected because the policy says actions should take effect immediately to the greatest extent possible.
- Informed consent and disclosure of resulting contract changes should reduce surprises and help customers understand whether they can return to their prior terms.
- Written confirmations create a record of what the customer selected and when to expect documentation.
- A prohibition on fees for accessing customer service removes that cost as a barrier to getting help.
- Flexible channel choices allow providers to use suitable mechanisms such as apps, websites, or email.

**Potential friction or uneven experiences**

- Providers retain flexibility over channel and design, so ease of use, feature parity, and processing speed may vary.
- The policy does not set a maximum confirmation time or a detailed service-level standard for when a change must complete.
- A customer may still encounter charges for an action itself; the no-fee rule concerns access to customer service, not every transaction.
- Customers who require accommodations may need accessible self-service options; the policy encourages accessibility but does not add detailed specifications in this proceeding.
- The mechanism may seek clarification when reasonably necessary, but the boundary between clarification and a required representative interaction is not quantified.

## Operational Impacts

The following are implementation implications inferred from the policy, not additional requirements expressly enumerated by the CRTC:

- **Digital product and service design:** Build or adapt self-service flows for plan changes and cancellation, including clear options, disclosures, consent capture, and confirmation of the selected action.
- **Provisioning and billing integration:** Connect the customer-facing mechanism to order management, provisioning, billing, and account systems so that submitted changes are accurately and promptly executed.
- **Confirmation and evidence:** Generate and deliver written confirmations; display the expected delivery timeframe; retain an auditable record of requests, consent, outcomes, and delivery status.
- **Customer support readiness:** Update scripts, escalation paths, and help content for failed, delayed, disputed, or unclear self-service actions. Ensure customers can follow up when a confirmation does not arrive.
- **Fees and billing controls:** Review customer-service fee practices and distinguish prohibited access fees from any separately chargeable service action, consistent with applicable CRTC determinations.
- **Accessibility and privacy:** Assess the mechanism against existing applicable accessibility and privacy obligations; include accommodation needs in design and support planning.
- **Compliance governance:** Assign ownership, test end-to-end flows, monitor completion and confirmation failures, and prepare for complaints or CRTC inquiries. The CRTC, rather than the CCTS, will administer this requirement.
- **Delivery planning:** The policy recognizes that implementation may require backend changes and gives providers until 26 April 2027 before enforcement begins (paras. 34, 39; p. 8).

## Risks

| Risk | Potential impact | Relevant policy response / consideration |
|---|---|---|
| Self-service flow is incomplete, confusing, or effectively funnels customers to a representative | Non-compliance and continued barriers to changing or cancelling plans; customer complaints | The mechanism must be easy to use and enable customers to act without a live representative, subject to reasonable clarification (paras. 13, 16). |
| Requested changes do not propagate accurately across systems | Incorrect billing, service interruption, disputes, and loss of customer trust | The policy expects actions to take effect immediately to the greatest extent possible and requires written confirmation (paras. 13, 29). |
| Customer does not understand contract consequences or cannot establish what was agreed | Unintended commitments, disputes, and complaints | The policy requires transparent, informed and express consent, relevant contract-change information, and written confirmation (paras. 14–15, 28–29). |
| Confirmation delivery fails or the expected timeframe is unclear | Customers may not know whether an action completed or when to follow up | Providers must state the expected confirmation timeframe in the mechanism and send written confirmation (para. 29). |
| Fraud or unintended changes through self-service | Unauthorized plan changes or customer harm | Providers raised security concerns; the CRTC considers security measures can be incorporated while meeting the requirement (paras. 9, 12). |
| Access fees continue under another label or are confused with action fees | Consumer detriment and potential non-compliance | No fees may be charged to access any type of customer service; action fees are a distinct issue and may still apply subject to other determinations (paras. 26–27). |
| Accessibility needs are not met in the chosen channel | Some customers may be unable to use self-service independently | Existing protections apply; the CRTC encourages accessible mechanisms and not relying exclusively on live support for customers needing accommodations (paras. 30–31). |
| Implementation is delayed or dependent systems are not ready by the enforcement date | Enforcement exposure and rushed deployment | Enforcement starts 26 April 2027; integration complexity and implementation time were noted in the proceeding (paras. 34, 39). |

## Assumptions

- The assessment uses the policy as issued on 24 April 2026; it does not account for later amendments, interpretations, or separate CRTC decisions.
- “Provider” means a telecommunications service provider within the policy’s and Act’s scope. The policy determines that the requirement applies to all service providers; it does not establish an exemption in this proceeding (para. 38).
- The organization assessing impact offers telecommunications service plans and therefore should determine its applicability rather than assume exemption.
- Existing accessibility, privacy, and other consumer-protection obligations continue to apply; this policy does not replace them (para. 30).
- Operational impacts such as system integration, testing, training, evidence retention, and monitoring are reasonable implementation considerations, not standalone requirements explicitly listed by the CRTC.
- The stated enforcement date is 26 April 2027; providers should verify the current legal and regulatory position before setting their final compliance plan.

## Open Questions

The policy establishes the core outcomes but leaves several implementation details to providers or subsequent clarification:

1. What objective usability or accessibility criteria will the CRTC use when assessing whether a mechanism is “easy to use”?
2. What situations qualify as reasonably requiring additional information to clarify customer intent, and when may that clarification involve a representative?
3. What constitutes “immediately” in operational cases where provisioning, billing cycles, or technical dependencies prevent real-time completion?
4. Is there a minimum or maximum acceptable confirmation timeframe, and what delivery channels or fallback procedures will be considered sufficient?
5. What records, retention periods, or evidence will providers be expected to maintain to demonstrate consent, execution, and confirmation?
6. How will the CRTC receive, route, and resolve complaints, and what compliance reporting or monitoring will it require from providers?
7. How should the no-fee-for-access prohibition be applied to existing fee schedules and bundled service/support arrangements, especially where a fee could be characterized as an action charge?
8. What detailed accessibility practices are expected under existing requirements for customers who cannot use a provider’s chosen digital mechanism?
9. How should the policy’s applicability be assessed for specific provider, reseller, business-account, or service-plan arrangements not expressly addressed in the assessment?
